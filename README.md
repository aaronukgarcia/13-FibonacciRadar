# The Spiral and the Wave
### Scale-Invariant Geometry for Broadband Electromagnetic and Acoustic Absorption

> "The logarithmic spiral is the only curve that is scale-invariant... As the wave frequency changes, the active part of the spiral simply shifts along the curve, but the absorption mechanism remains identical."  
> — *Wang, Hou & Chan, Scientific Reports (2019)*

## 📄 Overview
**Status:** Draft / Request for Comment (RFC)  
**Version:** 1.0 (01 Jan 2026)  
**Author:** Aaron Garcia

This repository hosts the white paper **"The Spiral and the Wave,"** which explores a theoretical framework for adapting electromagnetic stealth geometry to underwater acoustics. 

It asks a central question: **Can the scale-invariant properties of logarithmic spirals—demonstrated to absorb 95% of microwave energy across six octaves—be successfully transferred to acoustic waves in water?**

## 🎯 The Core Concept
Current stealth technology (both radar and sonar) typically relies on:
* **Platform shaping** (compromises hydrodynamics/aerodynamics).
* **Resonant coatings** (narrowband, frequency-specific).
* **Active cancellation** (complex, expensive, fragile).

**The proposed alternative:**
A materials-based approach using **Logarithmic Spiral Resonators (LSRs)**. Because the logarithmic spiral is uniquely scale-invariant (rotation = scaling), a single geometric motif can theoretically support broadband absorption without stacking multiple resonator types.

## ❓ The Central Question
In 2019, Wang, Hou, and Chan demonstrated that this geometry works for electromagnetics (6–37 GHz). This project seeks to determine if the mathematical analogy between **Maxwell’s equations (TM mode)** and **acoustic wave equations (shear-horizontal)** holds strong enough to allow this physics to transfer to the underwater domain.
